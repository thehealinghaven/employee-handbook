People leave. When you do, we'd like it to go well for you, for your clients, and for the practice.

## Notice

We ask for at least **four weeks' notice** from clinicians and two weeks from other roles. Clinicians need longer because clients need a real transition, not a surprise. Notice isn't legally required, and your employment is at will, but giving it is how you leave with your relationships and your reputation intact, and it affects whether you're eligible for rehire.

## How to resign

Tell Helen directly, in person or by video, then confirm in writing (email to Helen and the practice manager is fine) with your last day. Don't let the practice find out from a client or a colleague.

## During your notice period

- Keep seeing clients and keep documenting. Your notes must be complete by your last day.
- Work with Helen on transitioning every client. See [Return of property and client transition](/leaving/return-of-property-and-client-transition).
- Don't solicit clients to follow you, and don't take any client information. See [What survives employment](/leaving/what-survives-employment).
- Complete the offboarding checklist with the practice manager.

The practice may choose to shorten your notice period. If we do, you'll be paid through the date you actually stop working, and final pay timing follows the termination rule.

## If we end your employment

You'll be told directly, by Helen, with the reason where one applies. Client transition is handled by the practice. You'll receive final pay per [Final pay](/leaving/final-pay) and information about any benefits.

## Exit conversation

We'll ask for an exit conversation. It's optional, and it's your chance to tell us what we should know. We listen.

## References

The practice confirms dates of employment and title. Anything beyond that is at Helen's discretion and with your permission.
